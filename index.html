Islamul Haque

Postdoctoral Associate, Yale Research Initiative on Innovation and Scale (Y-RISE), Yale University

I am an applied microeconomist specializing in development and environmental economics. My research focuses on identifying and overcoming the frictions that constrain climate change adaptation and labor mobility in developing countries. I study how to design scalable solutions to these challenges and generate empirical evidence on their effectiveness and implementation at scale.

I received my PhD in economics from the University of Southern California. Much of my work combines large-scale field experiments and administrative data from Bangladesh.

Job Market Paper

Smooth Today, Poor Tomorrow: Floods and the Making of Chronic Poverty in Rural Bangladesh

Poor rural households smooth consumption through severe floods almost completely, yet three to seven years later the non-poor have rebuilt past their starting point while the poor remain 10 percent down in consumption and 21 points more likely to stay poor. The mechanism is financing: floods destroy the land value that collateralizes production credit.
